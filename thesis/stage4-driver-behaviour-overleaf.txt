Stage~3 (signals and intersections) finishes the network edits at full demand:
Webster timing for actuated signals; short links joined and lane continuations
or lifted lane-change bans where the default driver was stuck in both seeds;
and one OpenStreetMap pass at junctions where several vehicles gave up waiting
to give way (actuated signal if OSM has a light there, edge priorities from the
OSM road class). Signal programs are carried over connection by connection;
any new signal gets a Webster timing. After stage~3 the network and its signals
are fixed.

Stage~4 is only about how drivers act on that road. An evolutionary search
tunes car-following (Krauss), lane changing and overtaking (LC2013), junction
behaviour (impatience, minor-road time gaps, time-to-impatience), and en-route
rerouting (probability, period, and travel-time adaptation), on the mean of
seeds 23 and 42. Fitness weights teleports ten times more heavily than
vehicles that never enter, penalises collisions above the SUMO default driver,
and penalises travel times far from Google's on a fixed trip sample. Each round
starts from the best drivers so far and stops at zero teleports, after two
rounds without a new lowest count, or after four rounds.
